What are you protecting?

Written as IPTC Data Mining (PLUS vocabulary), W3C TDM reservation, XMP rights terms and, for code, SPDX-style header lines.

  • An Ed25519 key is generated in this browser and kept in its local storage. Your identity is the key's fingerprint. Export it to sign from another device.

  • Images: a keyed DCT mark on a 256-grid, blind-detectable, survives resizing, recompression, screenshots and colour edits (not heavy crops). Text: a zero-width fingerprint woven into whitespace plus a unique canary phrase that will surface if a model memorises your work.

  • Public calendars receive the signed manifest's 32-byte hash and return a pending receipt. A Bitcoin attestation becomes available after the calendars obtain sufficient confirmations; completion time is not guaranteed. A completed proof requires independent verification against a Bitcoin block header.

File contents stay in browser memory. Optional timestamp requests send only the manifest hash to public calendars. Reloading clears the inputs; your signing key stays in this browser until you remove it.

1 · Assert

Standards-based declarations are what compliant crawlers read and what regulators and courts look for. The EU text-and-data-mining opt-out is binding in the EU; ignoring an explicit, machine-readable reservation is the behaviour being litigated now.

2 · Prove

A signed manifest binds the content digest to the signing key. An OpenTimestamps proof can establish a time bound after calendar confirmation and verification against a Bitcoin block header. This browser checks proof encoding and manifest binding; it does not verify Bitcoin block headers.

3 · Detect

Strip the metadata and the watermark still says which protected work this is. The canary phrase is unique to your file: search model outputs for it.

What this does not do

  • It cannot physically stop a scraper that ignores declared permissions. It makes ignoring them deliberate, provable and attributable.
  • It cannot protect what is already public without these marks. Protect before publishing.
  • Heavy cropping, rotation and extreme warps defeat the image watermark. Deliberate Unicode normalisation removes the zero-width text fingerprint; the visible canary and the signed digest remain.
  • Your signing key lives in this browser. Export it and keep the export somewhere safe; losing it means new work is signed by a new identity.
